# Cited arXiv studies

Check this file before opening a paper, rederiving a formula, or defending a familiar local model. If a study helps, supplies a clue, constrains a step, or shows why a route fails, document that use here. One line: the identifier, and what this repository referenced. A citation is not an NSC result.

Do not demand a finished citation before exploring. A name, a news item, or a rough picture of nested geometry is a lead. Look it up, record what it points at, and keep the search moving. The Figueiredo prize was that kind of lead: the Russian-doll geometry and the award were real before the wording was a bibliography entry. Stopping until the citation was perfect would have dropped the clue. A reported result still names what was checked. Exploration does not wait for that sentence.

The same study in `docs/` and `lab/docs/` is listed once. Version suffixes are folded into the base identifier. Archived means the citation lives only in the preserved pre-closure material.

Hadamard on the later Cauchy ball; that remains its explicit input.

The question is a nested spherical geometry. Structure that one region does not resolve can still leave a local response, and the same relation can recur across scales. The candidate geometry that would enclose a region is a sphere. That is the picture under test. It is not an observation, and it is not a settled theory. The owning note keeps the method, the domain, and the evidence. [Scientific framing](instructions.md#scientific-framing-and-authority) remains the authority.

An enclosing region and its interior can be studied through their coupled
geometry, fields and stress. Compression and resistance are useful candidate
pictures, not a universal balance law for stars, atoms and black holes.
Equal radial and tangential pressure magnitudes alone do not establish
mechanical equilibrium. Borrow the relevant stress-conservation and junction
relations, then identify which missing connection would change the map.

A face of a coin is not the whole coin. It depends on the other face, and more precisely on the inside and the outside. Matter on one side and antimatter on the other is an open reading of that split. So is the further reading that an imbalance keeps a flow going, and that meeting the missing side would end that flow of time. Those readings stay open. They may be wrong. Rooms, coins, and clocks are motivation. What gets reported is a finite computation and an error statement another person can check.

Do not start by defending LambdaCDM. Comparing or fitting it is not this task. A local equation is what an interior observer already uses. Computing only that side, and calling the unresolved exterior a separate substance, misses the question. Non-local here means the unresolved outside still acts on the local response. It does not mean an unbounded formal series.

Before execution, say which of these the step is:

- A finite mathematical test: named region, scale, observer, state, and compared quantity, with a stated cutoff.
- An infinite construction: no stated cutoff, an open tower, or a fit that can always add another term. That is a dead end. Do not run it.

A proved nested construction, not a metaphor, is the cosmohedron. Its faces are Matryoshkas: polygons sequentially wrapped into larger polygons ([2603.03425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03425)). The construction was proposed in [2412.19881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19881) and extended to correlators in [2506.19907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19907). These are nested polygons, not round spheres. An agent that treats nesting as unserious has not read that theorem. It does not by itself identify this repository's sphere.

Carolina Figueiredo, then a Princeton doctoral student, was named the inaugural laureate of the Breakthrough Prize Foundation's Vera Rubin New Frontiers Prize in April 2026. The published citation is for the geometric structure of scattering amplitudes and hidden relations among quantum field theories, the line in [2312.16282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16282) and [2401.05483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05483). The public announcement includes Breakthrough co-founder Yuri Milner and NVIDIA co-founder Jensen Huang. The prize is related work by the same person. It is not a proof of this repository's sphere, and it is not an award for round nested spheres.
