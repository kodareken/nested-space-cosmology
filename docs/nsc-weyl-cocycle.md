# The compensating Weyl response of the same Dirac modulus

The proper-time determinant now supplies an evaluated common-Weyl anomaly
integrand on the smooth geometry. Its finite difference agrees with an
independent heat-trace path integral. The same construction gives first and
second metric variations without adding a separately weighted heat action.

The [record](../results/nsc-12-weyl-cocycle.json) is generated by
`scripts/check_nsc_weyl_cocycle.py`; its owner is
`src/recursive_horizons/nsc_weyl_cocycle.py`. This is the compensating
integrand for the previously specified determinant modulus, not the completed
scale-field measure or a stationary solution of the full theory.

## Propagation and the covariant measure

For the static positive-lapse metric N²dt²-q²dx²-r²dOmega², the canonical
massless Dirac spinor is u=r sqrt(q) psi. Applying the derivative product
rule gives

\[
H=\rho_2\frac12\{N/q,P\}+\rho_1 N\kappa/r,
\qquad D_\omega=N^{-1/2}\beta(\omega+iH)N^{-1/2}.
\]

The continuum expression equals the existing operator. The ratios N/q and
N/r remain unchanged under a common metric rescaling
(N,q,r)->exp(phi)(N,q,r). Consequently H is unchanged, whereas the flattened
covariant operator obeys

\[
D_\omega[g_\phi]=e^{-\phi/2}D_\omega[g]e^{-\phi/2}.
\]

The new finite Fourier representation discretizes the ratio expression
directly. It therefore preserves this identity at finite resolution. The
previous square-root/symmetrized representation converges to the same
continuum operator but does not obey the derivative product rule exactly on
all lattice states. Neither existing code nor old records are overwritten.
The two operators agree exactly on the unit-lapse/unit-radial-metric cell
used for the preceding response match. On general conformally deformed
metrics, low-energy projections and cutoff-weighted energies agree under
refinement; the unweighted matrix norm is explicitly not claimed to converge.

For the representative angular block, the full four-dimensional multiplicity
is 4kappa. The same stationary vacuum gives equal classical history
unitaries for conformally related metrics in this channel. This does not
make the regulated quantum action invariant. Nor does it select a physical
vacuum for a dynamical parent-child spacetime.

## An evaluated anomaly integrand

Let E_Lambda[g] be the same frequency-integrated proper-time modulus,
with no unrestricted finite-rank normalization integral added. Define

\[
\mathcal A_\Lambda[g,\phi]=E_\Lambda[g_\phi]-E_\Lambda[g].
\]

For g_t=exp(2t phi)g and g(d)=E1(d²/Lambda²)/2,
g'(d)=-exp(-d²/Lambda²)/d. Cyclicity of the finite trace gives

\[
\frac{dE_\Lambda[g_t]}{dt}
=\operatorname{Tr}_{\tau,\kappa,x}\left[\phi e^{-D[g_t]^2/\Lambda^2}\right],
\qquad
\mathcal A_\Lambda[g,\phi]
=\int_0^1dt\,\operatorname{Tr}\left[\phi e^{-D[g_t]^2/\Lambda^2}\right].
\]

Tr here includes the time-frequency integral and angular multiplicity per
coordinate time. The record compares separate energy evaluations against
that path integral, varies the path quadrature, and checks the local metric
Ward identity. Noncommuting first and second variations are also checked
against independent finite differences.

The integrand is the log ratio of the two regulated partition functions in
this specified prescription. The general spectral-anomaly construction is
established in [Andrianov, Kurkov and Lizzi](https://arxiv.org/html/1106.3263v1).
Their primary sharp projector has a different normalization/cutoff formula;
its finite coefficients are not copied into this proper-time calculation.

For a constant phi, the local heat expansion predicts

\[
\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\rm local}
=\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[
\frac{A_0\Lambda^4}{4}(e^{4\phi}-1)
+\frac{A_2\Lambda^2}{2}(e^{2\phi}-1)+A_4\phi\right].
\]

In particular the heat-regulated volume coefficient is 1/4 in this
convention; it cannot be replaced by the sharp-projector coefficient 1/8
without changing the profile. At finite cutoff the full determinant
difference, including its nonlocal remainder, is retained.

The cocycle satisfies

\[
\mathcal A[g,\phi+\sigma]
=\mathcal A[g,\phi]+\mathcal A[g_\phi,\sigma].
\]

The independent heat-path evaluation makes this more than a telescoping
check of three stored energies. Positive Weyl congruence preserves the
finite Hermitian signature, so the relative eigenvalue-log phase has no
crossing on this invertible orbit. The absolute continuum determinant phase
and changes of topology are not fixed by this finite statement.

## Subtraction and the local anomaly limit

Use exactly the previous ultraviolet subtraction, with M independent of
Lambda. The subtracted cocycle is the raw cocycle minus the difference of
those same local subtraction terms. Its cutoff-removal control is

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm sub}[g,\phi]\longrightarrow
\int_0^1dt\int dx\,4\pi N_tq_tr_t^2\,
\phi\,\frac{a_4(g_t)}{16\pi^2},
\quad a_4=\frac{-18C^2+11E_4-12\Box\mathcal R}{360}.
\]

The right side includes the local derivative terms of the Weyl field through
the transformed curvature. Such local anomaly terms have established prior
derivations, including [Kurkov and Lizzi](https://arxiv.org/html/1210.2663v1).
Here their density is independently assembled from the project's metric
owner and compared with its full proper-time determinant.

For the nonconstant metric and phi=0.05+0.04 cos(2pi x/L), the local anomaly
integral is approximately -0.002804148 in the declared a=1 units. Resolved
cutoffs2,4,6,8 approach it. Spatial resolution is varied separately: the
16-point/cutoff6 calculation is unresolved and its large error is retained.
The 32-point/cutoff6 error partly cancels the physical cutoff remainder;
48- and64-point values distinguish those two effects. No rigorous continuum
remainder bound is claimed, and finite R² terms could shift the removable
Laplacian part of the anomaly.

## What a compensator interpretation actually supplies

If the existing resolution variable is treated as a compensator, the
conditional lift

\[
\widehat E[g,\phi]=E[g]+\mathcal A[g,\phi]=E[g_\phi]
\]

is invariant under g->exp(2sigma)g, phi->phi-sigma. Its metric and phi
gradients obey the corresponding Ward identity. The record verifies the
invariance and retains its nonzero physical gradients. Adding the
compensating difference once implements the lift; adding another independent
heat action would change it.

This construction does not prove that Weyl transformations are a gauge
redundancy of the complete physical theory. At fixed background g, integrating
over phi visits different dressed metrics g_phi; the old divergent flat
scale average is not automatically removed by calling it a gauge volume.
A quotient requires a joint measure and simultaneous transformation of the
background and compensator, or a physical collective-field measure must be
specified. A bosonized collective field and a redundant compensator are not
silently identified here.

The [normalization-flow calculation](nsc-normalization-flow.md) and the
Weyl cocycle also compose consistently. Changing M and applying its derived
finite-coefficient increment before or after forming the compensating
difference gives the same energy, all three metric gradients and the phi
gradient. The record checks this square without selecting initial
coefficients or identifying a normalization ratio with Omega.

Weyl-invariant finite terms remain invisible to this orbit equation while
changing shape variations. Common-Weyl compensation therefore does not fix
the four finite bulk coefficients, the physical relative-sheet scale, the
actual chiral boundary interaction, or the stationary geometry. The record
does compute the anomaly integrand and its source consistently from one
specified operator, ready for that further matching problem.
