# MMP return stress beside the classical RN throat

This note fixes what the Maldacena–Milekhin–Popov construction is allowed to
mean for the classical Reissner–Nordström branch. It is a derivation, not a
campaign. It does not rerun the historical coefficient gates, does not evolve
the neutral parent, and does not block that parent. The neutral prototype
remains the pending production path.

The active asymptotically flat branch uses the native bulk term of the
[RN reference](nsc-rn-reference.md),

\[
S=\int\sqrt{|g|}\,\bigl[-A R_L-C_F F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigr],
\]

with the laboratory Riemann convention stated there,
\(R^a{}_{bcd}=\partial_c\Gamma^a{}_{db}-\partial_d\Gamma^a{}_{cb}+\cdots\) and
\(R_{bd}=R^a{}_{bad}\). There is no cosmological term:
\(V_4=0\). Older notes that write \(A R\) mean the opposite scalar
\(R_E=-R_L\) of the [compact matching](nsc-compact-matching.md), so
\(A R_E=-A R_L\). This note keeps the native label. A general declared \(V\)
can appear only on a separate future branch.

The return-stress owner may keep declared renormalized values of \(A\) and
\(C_F\). That permission does not insert \(V_4\) into the asymptotically flat
solution and does not change it. Historical coefficient inputs keep the scope
recorded in the [embedding gate](nsc-mmp-embedding.md) and the
[vacuum-coefficient gate](nsc-mmp-vacuum-owner.md). They are not a
prerequisite for the classical branch. \(G_N=1/(16\pi A)\) and
\(g_4^2=1/(4C_F)\). Forces already present in the native action are counted
once. No four-dimensional Weyl pole is added.

## Conditional null energy is a test-field statement

Two primary statements bound the null energy condition here.

Tóth, [arXiv:1509.02878](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02878), Classical and
Quantum Gravity 33 (2016) 115012, evaluates a classical Dirac test field on a
fixed extremal dyonic Kerr–Newman background, including the massless field on
a nonrotating charged hole. The Einstein–Hilbert stress of that test field
can violate the null energy condition. The horizon flux computed in that
approximation is the consequence of that violation. The paper records that
this does not contradict the weak cosmic censorship conjecture, because the
censorship hypotheses under test assume the null energy condition.

Natário, Queimada and Vicente,
[arXiv:1601.06809](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06809), Classical and Quantum
Gravity 33 (2016) 175002, prove the conditional theorem: a test field whose
stress satisfies the null energy condition at the event horizon, and whose
flux obeys their boundary condition at infinity, cannot destroy an extremal
Kerr–Newman or Kerr–Newman–AdS horizon. Their horizon inequality is
\(\Delta M\ge \Omega\Delta J+\Phi\Delta Q\).

A nonlinear cosmic-censorship statement would be a property of a solution of
the coupled Einstein–matter system. Both papers are test-field statements.
A negative null flux of a classical Dirac packet is state data on a frozen
background. It is not the action coefficient \(V\), and it is not the Casimir
stress below. The classical branch does not fit packet flux as vacuum energy.

## The classical throat carries positive Maxwell energy

The extremal near-horizon geometry of the native Einstein–Maxwell theory is
the equal-radius product \(AdS_2\times S^2\). The [RN reference](nsc-rn-reference.md)
contracts that theory in the laboratory Riemann convention and obtains
\(R_L=0\) on Reissner–Nordström, with the executable geometric charge

\[
r_Q^2=\frac{C_F q_{\rm geom}^2}{4A}.
\]

Here \(q_{\rm geom}\) is that owner’s flux parameter, not the prose label
\(2\pi q_{\rm geom}\). For the monopole bundle of the charged note the
orthonormal strength is \(\mathcal B=|q_{\rm geom}|/(2r_Q^2)\) on the sphere
of radius \(r_Q\), and the same identity reads
\(r_Q^2=C_F q_{\rm geom}^2/(4A)\). Because \(A>0\) and \(C_F>0\), this charge
square is positive. The active branch keeps \(V_4=0\), so the throat is
supported by that Maxwell term. A near-extremal single horizon remains a
black-hole throat of this classical solution. Negative vacuum energy is not
part of it.

## Negative Casimir is the horizonless return, allocated once

Maldacena, Milekhin and Popov,
[arXiv:1807.04726](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04726), construct a different
solution of Einstein–Maxwell theory plus charged massless fermions. A single
extremal magnetic hole still has the classical \(AdS_2\times S^2\) throat.
Joining two such throats into global \(AdS_2\) removes the horizon. Their
section 5.2 records that this horizonless joining requires negative energy.
That energy is the Casimir energy of the lowest Landau channels moving on the
closed magnetic field lines, including the two-dimensional conformal anomaly
already present in their induced stress.

Each lowest channel is localized on one field line. The line leaves one
mouth, crosses the exterior, enters the other mouth, and returns through the
throat, so its topology is one circle. The mark \(\Gamma=1\) is an allocation
aperture for that existing induced stress: the \(|q_{\rm mag}|\) channels are
counted once, and the two mouths are ends of the same lines. It is not a new
copy of the common action placed beside \(-A R_L-C_F F^2\).

On a throat coordinate of length \(L=\pi\ell\), with antiperiodic spin
structure along the circle, their decomposition is

\[
E_{\rm flat\ strip}=-\frac{|q_{\rm mag}|}{24}\frac{\pi}{L},
\]
\[
E_{\rm wormhole}
=+\frac{|q_{\rm mag}|}{24}\frac{\pi}{L}
-\frac{|q_{\rm mag}|}{12}\frac{2\pi}{L}
=-\frac{|q_{\rm mag}|}{8\ell}.
\]

The first piece of \(E_{\rm wormhole}\) cancels the flat-strip Casimir on pure
\(AdS_2\). The remainder is one negative term. When the exterior segments of
the same lines are retained, the flat piece becomes one integral over those
lines, which is their equation (5.44). That integral is the same aperture
\(\Gamma=1\), still not a second bulk term. The Wilson line sits at the
antiperiodic minimum because that choice minimizes this energy. The resulting
null-null component is negative along the non-achronal circles that wrap the
cylinder.

This quantum stress is admitted only for that horizonless traversable
return. It is not a source on the classical parent, whose domain is the
ingoing chart with horizons, pure-outflow excision, and a nonperiodic
absorbing outer boundary. The classical chart has no closed field-line
circle on which to place this Casimir.

The old partial Dirac coefficients, the stored \(V_{\rm full}\) mismatch, and
the historical heat-kernel Maxwell term stay in their gates. They are not
added on top of \(E_{\rm wormhole}\). A later wave may read declared
renormalized \(A\) and \(C_F\). It does not tune \(V_4\) to manufacture the
minus sign, and it does not change the asymptotically flat solution, whose
cosmological coefficient remains \(0\).

## Open owner

`derive_nsc_rn_parent` and of the neutral classical evolution.

\[

\text{ on one horizonless magnetic return.}}
\]

That owner evaluates the existing induced null stress of the
\(|q_{\rm mag}|\) lowest channels on the single return, at the antiperiodic
Wilson minimum. The subscript \(\Gamma=1\) only marks that one allocation.
Its geometric inputs are the return length \(\ell\), with \(L=\pi\ell\) in

The owner does not run an old source or ultraviolet gate, does not fit a
classical packet flux as vacuum energy, and does not gate the neutral
Reissner–Nordström prototype. At most two later derivation waves may touch
it, and those waves share at most one numerical CPU-hour. This wave is the
algebra above.
