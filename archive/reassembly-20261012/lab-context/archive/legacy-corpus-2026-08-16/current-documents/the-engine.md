## 0. The Theory in One Paragraph

## 2. The Chain

### 2.1 Geometry: Every Black Hole Interior IS a Universe

Inside \(r_s = 2GM/c^2\) the Schwarzschild metric's coefficients swap sign. Rename \(T = r\), \(R = t\):

\[

\]

This is the **Kantowski–Sachs metric** — a homogeneous, anisotropic, contracting cosmology. \(T\) runs from the horizon to the center; spatial volume shrinks as \(T^2\). Every black hole contains a universe in contraction. **Status: exact differential geometry. Undisputable.**

### 2.2 The Collapse Clock

The collapse does not happen instantly. It is free fall through a gradient, and free fall takes time:

\[
\tau_{\rm collapse} = \int_{r_s}^{0} \frac{dr}{\sqrt{r_s/r - 1}} = \frac{\pi GM}{c^3}

### 2.3 The Wall at the Bottom

Why doesn't the collapse reach zero size? Because resolution costs energy, and energy has gravity. A probe resolving a distance \(\Delta x\) needs energy \(E \sim \hbar c/\Delta x\), whose own Schwarzschild radius is:

\[
r_{\rm probe} = \frac{2GE}{c^4} = \frac{2\ell_P^2}{\Delta x}

Demanding the probe not be a black hole itself gives:

\[
\boxed{\Delta x \ge \sqrt{2}\,\ell_P}

At \(\Delta x = 10^{-36}\) m the probe's horizon is 522× larger than the probe. Pushing deeper **enlarges the wall**. The singularity of classical GR — a point of infinite density — is revealed as what it always was: the place where the theory's own measuring apparatus forms a horizon. Quantum mechanics and gravity are not in conflict here; the wall is where they become the same statement. **The bounce is compulsory, not optional.**
