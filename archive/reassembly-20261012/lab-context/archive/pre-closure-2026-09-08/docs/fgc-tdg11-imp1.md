## Exact bounded evaluation

The implementation qualifies an exact Bernstein sufficient-proof path
with the existing dual-exact localizers as an unresolved-case fallback. This
is a new evaluator of the same corrected cubics, not a change to the order
threshold or a claim that an approximate extremum is exact.
The MSEL1 diagnostic required both localizers on every measured channel;
PREF1 does not itself qualify this sufficient-proof shortcut. IMP1 uses
a new evaluator/schema identity and independently qualifies its certificates.
Fast decisions report no invented localizer counts or survivor identities.
`fallback_calls` counts invocations of the existing dual-route adapter, not
the number of individual polynomial-localizer calls inside it.

For a Hermite segment, form Bernstein controls directly:

```text
(z_left, z_left + h*f_left/3, z_right - h*f_right/3, z_right).
```

Dyadic de Casteljau restriction and subtraction produce the complete two
outer/medium and four medium/fine difference cubics per row. Their convex
hulls give exact upper bounds. Exact evaluations at
`0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1` give lower bounds. A nonzero cubic cannot vanish at all
five points; an interval merely containing zero must not become a fabricated
exact-zero or enclosure-debit-only pass. Adaptive subdivision may tighten
only certified bounds. Unresolved comparisons fall back to both exact
localizers. Intersect their agreed enclosure with the retained Bernstein
enclosure; an empty intersection is a typed disagreement, not a choice of
the more favorable route. Malformed input or exhausted resources stop
without acceptance. Raw visibility may remain an explicitly unresolved
enclosure; it does not control admission.

For the ledger, retain `U12_Bernstein + R_fine`, even when the admission
fallback obtains a tighter upper bound. Record the gate upper, the Bernstein
upper, and their nonnegative enclosure difference separately. This debit
dominates the selected remedy's required `U12_gate + R_fine`; enclosure slack
is not called roundoff or a physical error estimate.
The unchanged-slope Hermite identity bounds the actual fine path relative to
the corrected medium path. It does not bound the raw fine/medium difference
without the medium accumulation term, or establish global PDE stability.
A larger conservative debit can tighten a later error-budget stop; identical
production behavior is not claimed.

Recorded binary64 data are dyadic. RK weights and the direct Hermite controls
place corrected endpoints, differences, and debit in `(1/3) Z[1/2]`.
Dyadic restriction, subtraction, absolute value, maximum and addition preserve
that set. Thus this new debit introduces no stationary-root denominator
primes into repeated accumulation. Bit-size, row, subdivision, history, and
serialization bounds are nevertheless required: restricted denominator
primes alone do not prove finite resource use. Resource bounds are prospective
implementation limits, never fitted scientific thresholds.

Prospective default limits are 2,044 owned rows, 32,768 numerator/denominator
bits, additional Bernstein depth 12, and at most 32,768 subdivisions per

rejection history to 65,536 records and a complete checkpoint to 16 MiB.
Smaller limits are injectable resource-failure controls; a future live owner
must bind its exact settings. A subdivision work limit can select the fixed
fallback; it cannot itself supply a scientific decision.

## State, ledger and restart boundary

Use new IMP1 prepared, committed, retry, ledger and checkpoint types. Never
construct a TDG6 prepared/committed record with C1 labels. Existing HLT16,
PROTO18, TDG6 and TDG7 files remain sealed and unchanged.

The new ledger keeps the complete authenticated inherited TDG6 snapshot as a
frozen sibling. Its original time, binary64 debit bits, counts and canonical
rejection history never advance or reset. IMP1 owns separate current time,
accepted/retry counts, exact rational accumulated debit, and canonical
`rejected_TDG11_IMP1_temporal_admission` records. A combined diagnostic debit
may add the inherited binary64 values as exact dyadic rationals; it does not
rewrite or masquerade as the inherited ledger.

Fresh and retry preparation have disjoint public entrypoints. A fresh call
rejects an active retry ledger. A retry call consumes the exact preceding
rejection, its accepted-boundary identity, and its halved cap through the
TDG7 stage-safe lattice. It cannot accept replacement time, cap or ledger
arguments. The durable sink must succeed before a successor ledger is
returned. Sink failure, source/health stops and resource stops do not spend a
temporal retry or advance a real state.
Each canonical rejection includes the complete preparation SHA-256, event
target, accepted boundary, assessment SHA-256, channel order and nonadmitted
channels. Equal numerical families prepared under different targets or
tracer/guard receipts cannot exchange genuine rejection evidence. Monitor
stage counts must equal their next serial; nonempty monitors must match the
accepted time, while the untouched zero-count monitor retains its legitimate
zero-time sentinel.

The lattice is planned before calling source, projector, tracer or state
hooks. Preparation builds independent 1/2/4 shadows and rechecks the real
state, monitor, causal ledger, tracer history and coordinates. Commit rechecks
the accepted boundary, validated proposal family and admission receipt, then
adopts only the actual four-substep endpoint and its ledgers. Late tracer or
adoption failure restores the complete prior monitor, causal and tracer
state. No diagnostic endpoint or corrected endpoint is serialized.

IMP1's checkpoint extension is a strict canonical in-memory codec, not a
store writer or a complete campaign cursor. The future PROTO19/HLT17 owner
must authenticate the stored fresh/retry discriminator, source/operator
closure, member metadata, journal and atomic checkpoint. The only allowed
future production origin is the synchronized sealed HLT15 `t=23/16` state,
not RCV3 or any QA/MSEL endpoint. Kernel construction of a snapshot alone
does not establish historical origin authentication.

## Failure forecast

- A mathematically loose or malformed enclosure can misclassify a channel.
  Independent exact controls, explicit zero identities, route intersections
  and forced-fallback tests distinguish this from a true order nonpass.
- A retry/codec/adoption mismatch can erase inherited debt or partially
  advance state. Bitwise snapshot round trips, strict event schemas and
  injected post-mutation failures test the owning state layer directly.
- Exact construction may remain too costly, or exceed a prospective bound.
  Report the resource stop or measured cost; do not omit channels, lower
  precision, relax `p >= 3/2`, retry informally, or open the campaign.

Even successful IMP1 qualification leaves first-event progression, eligible
GR-0 calibration, SGB-L ownership, DEF1, holdout, COL1 and ROB1 outstanding.
