# GMF-1: spherical matching and flux closure

GMF-1 is a controlled **obstruction and local-shell diagnostic**, not a global black-hole-to-child-universe solution. It keeps spherical symmetry, zero charge/spin, finite parent mass, common late-time causal cone, and no entropy-saturation or fitted dark-energy law.

## EC-1 reduced homogeneous finite-cap candidate

The current release retains a deliberately reduced Einstein--Cartan/Weyssenhoff-fluid
candidate before, not instead of, GMF-1B. Under homogeneous/isotropic spin
averaging, a closed finite cap has the stated first integral

\[
\dot a^2=-1+\frac{A}{a^2}-\frac{B}{a^4},\qquad A,B>0.
\]

The positive energy-density and torsion-effective terms scale respectively as
`a^-4` and `-a^-6`. A separately conserved fermion number density in the spin
average scales as `n_f proportional to a^-3`. The latter has `w=+1` even though
its effective density is negative; it is not a vacuum component and does not
supply late-time `w=-1` dark energy. When `A^2>4B`, the finite roots are

\[
a_\pm^2=\frac{A\pm\sqrt{A^2-4B}}2.
\]

The lower root is a bounce (`ddot a>0`); all homogeneous curvature/stress
invariants are finite there because `a_- > 0`. With the CCT-1 null-normal
convention, one and the same interior FLRW metric can pass through trapped,
normal near-bounce, and anti-trapped round-sphere regions. This reduced
sequence is a local causal control, not an exterior/interior global geometry.

For a cap at constant `chi_b`, `R_b=a sin(chi_b)`, the FLRW Misner--Sharp
boundary mass and its exact pressure-work identity are

\[
M_{{\rm MS},b}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_{\rm eff}R_b^3,
\qquad
\dot M_{{\rm MS},b}=-4\pi R_b^2p_{\rm eff}\dot R_b.
\]

The torsion term has nonzero pressure, so this homogeneous boundary mass
drifts. A static Schwarzschild exterior has fixed mass and cannot remain a
smooth match without a pressure taper, an exterior flux, or a resolved layer.
This is a boundary-closure failure, not evidence that every Einstein--Cartan
completion fails. It supplies no global child, topology change, external
energy transfer `Q`, dark-energy mechanism, or relative-cone/varying-`c`
signal. The source reductions are [Popławski (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12190)
and [Ziaie et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-014-3154-2).

## GMF-1B-PF1: two preflight exclusions, not a transition

Before a radial solver is authorized, the proposed matter closure must be able
to reverse the relevant null focusing and possess a regular causal principal
part. A smooth canonical scalar minimally coupled to ordinary Einstein gravity
fails the first restricted gate. For every metric-null `k^a`,

\[
T_{ab}k^ak^b=(k^a\nabla_a\Phi)^2\geq0,
\qquad
R_{ab}k^ak^b=8\pi G T_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0.
\]

For the twist-free radial congruences used in spherical symmetry,

\[
\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}=-\frac{\theta^2}{2}
-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}-R_{ab}k^ak^b\leq0.
\]

Thus this action cannot give metric-null defocusing through a trapped-to-
expanding bounce. This is a statement about the restricted canonical
Einstein--scalar model, not an assertion that CCT-1's regular closed de Sitter
control is a black-hole transition, nor a no-go for modified gravity,
noncanonical fields, or spinor/torsion closures.

The particular thermal averaged EC fluid used by EC-1 fails the second gate
if promoted naively to radial material. With `z=B/(Aa^2)`,

\[
c_s^2=\frac{dp}{d\rho}=\frac{2-9z}{3(2-3z)},
\qquad z_{\rm bounce}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4B/A^2}}2\in(1/2,1).
\]

It has `c_s^2<0` for `2/9<z<2/3`, a pole at `z=2/3` where `rho+p=0`, and
`c_s^2>1` for `z>2/3`. Every route from the radiation-like low-`z` regime to
the lower turning point encounters one or more of these failures. This rejects
the *averaged perfect-fluid closure*, not EC theory. Full EC--Dirac evolution
and other action-derived, tapered closures remain open and must derive their
own characteristic cones and constraints.

The constructive GMF-1B target is only a finite expanding closed-FLRW-like
`B^3` cap. A later complete boundaryless `S^3` Cauchy slice cannot arise from
a noncompact finite-mass parent under smooth, connected, globally hyperbolic,
fixed-topology evolution. A proposal for a complete child must therefore
identify which premise changes; it may not infer `S^3` from a cap. Finally, a
candidate that contains an inner/Cauchy horizon has an additional stability
gate: it must show either that no such horizon occurs or that counter-streaming
perturbations leave quasi-local mass and curvature bounded under refinement.
Mass-inflation analyses make that a necessary test, not an automatic failure
of every regular-bounce proposal.

## No-shell mass gate

For a Kottler parent,

\[
f_+(R)=1-\frac{2GM}{R}-\frac{\Lambda_+R^2}{3},
\qquad
m_{{\rm MS},+}=M+\frac{\Lambda_+R^3}{6G}.
\]

For the existing CCT-1 pure de-Sitter child in closed FLRW slicing,

\[
a(\tau)=L\cosh(\tau/L),\qquad
\Lambda_-=3/L^2,\qquad
m_{{\rm MS},-}=\frac{\Lambda_-R^3}{6G}.
\]

Continuity without a surface layer requires equal Misner--Sharp mass:

\[
M=\frac{(\Lambda_--\Lambda_+)R_\Sigma^3}{6G}.
\]

Thus identical vacuum constants require `M=0`. With unequal constants, a nonzero `M` can balance only one fixed radius, not an evolving interface. GMF-1 reports this as a direct no-shell obstruction; it does not infer that every possible ultraviolet completion fails.

## Exact finite-mass regression

The Oppenheimer--Snyder dust ball is the positive control. For a closed dust FLRW cap at constant `chi_b`,

\[
R_b=a\sin\chi_b,\qquad M=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho R_b^3,
\]

\[
\dot a^2+1=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho a^2,
\qquad
\dot R_b^2=\frac{2GM}{R_b}-\sin^2\chi_b.
\]

It is an established exact smooth finite-mass Schwarzschild/closed-FLRW
match, but the executable regression here checks its reduced Friedmann, mass,
and boundary-geodesic identities rather than independently reconstructing both
induced metrics and extrinsic curvatures.  The solution reaches `a=0`; it is
singular and not a bounce or a child-universe construction.
The executable record does not merely attach that label: it evaluates
`rho proportional to a^-3` and `R=8 pi G rho` at `a` and `a/100`, obtaining
a factor of one million in each.  Their divergence as `a -> 0` is the
curvature/stress singularity excluded by the requested regular transition.

## Null expansions and local shell

In ingoing Eddington--Finkelstein parent coordinates, GMF-1 uses future null normals with `k_+ . k_- = -2`:

\[
\theta_+=\frac{\sqrt2 f_+}{R},\qquad
\theta_-=-\frac{2\sqrt2}{R},\qquad
\theta_+\theta_-=-\frac{4f_+}{R^2}.
\]

It checks normal, marginal, and trapped Schwarzschild spheres and separately calls the established CCT-1 child classifier. It never declares those separate samples connected.

For a prescribed timelike Israel shell,

\[
\epsilon_-\sqrt{\dot R^2+f_-}-\epsilon_+\sqrt{\dot R^2+f_+}=\kappa R,
\qquad \kappa=4\pi G\sigma,
\]

and the squared potential is

\[
\dot R^2+V(R)=0,\qquad
V=f_+-\frac{(f_--f_+-\kappa^2R^2)^2}{4\kappa^2R^2}.
\]

GMF-1 accepts a diagnostic state only when the **unsquared** equation and the squared potential both pass, preserving the orientation signs `epsilon_±`. The deterministic at-rest state is intentionally checked for whether it is static via `V=V'=0`; it is not assumed stable. The derivative is a fixed-`sigma`, fixed-`kappa` diagnostic, not a general shell-stability calculation: a physical stability result also needs a surface equation of state, the resulting `sigma(R)`, flux law, orientation/timelike admissibility in a neighborhood, and derivative convergence. The shell conservation identity, with `lambda` the shell proper time and the flux jump oriented by the chosen child-to-parent normal, is

\[
\frac{d(\sigma A)}{d\lambda}+p_\Sigma\frac{dA}{d\lambda}
=-A[T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu n^\nu].
\]

For constant tension `p_Sigma=-sigma` and vacuum bulk flux, it closes with zero residual but does not derive a homogeneous child exchange `Q`. A thin Israel layer has distributional curvature, so it cannot meet an everywhere-finite curvature-invariant transition gate.

Here `f_-` is a local static-patch representation of the same de-Sitter
geometry used by CCT-1.  Its use in the shell equation does not turn the shell
interior into an entire boundaryless closed-FLRW `S^3` child.  The static gate
also requires the original oriented junction equation at zero velocity; a
root of the squared potential alone is never accepted.

## Closure status and nonclaims

The artifact sets all of the following false: `global_solution_constructed`, `child_topology_proven`, and `derived_exchange_law`. No surface action, smooth finite-thickness action, global trajectory, or perturbative stability theory is supplied. A spherical interface bounds a closed-FLRW **patch**, not by itself a complete boundaryless `S^3` child slice.
