## Abstract

Nested-Space Cosmology asks whether one inherited frequency spectrum, carried
by an enclosing region, can describe what we separately call particles, waves,
matter, antimatter, dark gravity, and expanding space. This statement takes the
geometric question first. Which relations exist depends on the space, so a
nested description is taken from the enclosing side; and any shape other than
the sphere requires more from the two measured forces that hold a region. A
sphere encloses without seams and carries its own clock, its own two ends, and a
horizon where the outward path ends. The programme's measured results are
collected here: a completed finite parent-child pair; a finite spherical
realization in which a regional difference drives transfer, geometric feedback,
maintained localization, and a measurable local response under one action; a
cone map in which the local speed of light stays one while the chart value is
carried by the shift; a one-way signature across the measured trap that is
strict for the deepest family case and for the intermediate case on the
horizon-reaching domain, with a low-frequency exception remaining for the
heaviest; a room
whose bottom and top ends are set by the local space itself; a comparison in
which the programme's chain nesting is the Schroeder skeleton of the proved
nested-polygon family; and an inward march that decides the 2005 regularity
criterion on the domain that reaches the inner horizon, where the compressive
slope does not flatten. It also states what is not established: no value of c or
Lambda is derived, no continuum state-error certificate exists, renewal is
false, and the paused incoming-gate campaign is not resumed.

## 1. The question, three months in

The first version of this programme was organized by a spectrum. A room was a
locally complete spacetime with its own clocks, rulers, and resolved spectrum;
a black-hole boundary was the handoff at which a compressed parent gradient
continues in a child room with a new absolute register and the same
dimensionless law. That framing produced a 43-page foundation, a finite
operator theorem, and a library of calculations. It is preserved byte-for-byte
as version 0.26.0 and it is still the mathematical archive of the programme.

Three months of working inside it changed the emphasis. The spectral statement
was never the whole claim: what kept recurring, in every calculation that
survived scrutiny, was an *enclosure* relation. A region has an outside that
acts on it; an unresolved exterior leaves a response; a compressed interior
pushes back; an interface decides which way anything can travel. The object
that carries all of those properties at once is not a spectrum. It is a shape
with an inside.

So the question this statement puts first is:

> What is the shape that can hold a universe inside a universe, and what can
> that shape actually do?

The rest of this document answers in three parts: why the question is
geometric at all (§2), why the sphere is the candidate (§3), and what has
actually been measured about the capabilities of that enclosure (§5, §6),
followed by the limits (§7) and the next question (§8).

## 2. The physical premises

Two things are assumed and one is exact. They are separated here because a
reader is entitled to reject the assumptions before reading the results.

### 2.1 The description is taken from the enclosing side

Which relations exist depends on the space: there are no knots in two
dimensions, the cross product exists only in three and seven, and spinors need a
metric with a signature. A nested description is therefore taken from the
enclosing region rather than from the region described. A partial change of
frame relabels the local relations; only a complete step out of the local frame
exposes the enclosing ones. In this paper *non-local* means exactly that: the
unresolved outside still acts on the local response. It does not mean an
unbounded series.

### 2.2 The sphere is the least demanding equilibrium

Any other shape requires more from the two forces. The two forces are the
measured pair of §6 — the compressive inner contribution and the resisting
outer one — and a sphere is the configuration in which they need supply nothing
beyond isotropic stress. Departure from sphericity requires shear, and the
shear has to be supplied. The classical theory of equilibrium figures makes this
sharp: an isolated, non-rotating, self-consistent fluid body has the sphere as
its equilibrium figure, while a sustained anisotropic driver — rotation,
external shear, magnetic stress — admits non-spherical equilibria such as
spheroids and ellipsoids (Lichtenstein 1933; Chandrasekhar 1969).

This is a statement about which geometries survive, and it is testable. If the
sphere is selected by cost, then measured anisotropy should track the presence
of sustained anisotropic drivers: it can grow while a driver acts, and it
should decay in an undriven, late-time sector. Section 8 states the test and
the observation that already bears on it.

### 2.3 What is inherited

A sphere has no surface landmarks, so if inheritance required markings it would
be the worst available container. What an enclosure provides instead is a
discrete spectrum — a countable set of modes — and what survives transfer
between rooms is the dimensionless ratio structure of that spectrum, not its
absolute register.

The exact statement of inherited law is the relation

$$
\mathcal T_\Theta^*\mathbb D_\Theta=\mathbb D_\Theta ,
$$

read as: the operator of a room, transported to the equivalent depth of another
room, is the same operator. In the finite form used by the current companion,
for a Hermitian prototype $H$, a link $B$, a scale $\Omega>0$ and finite depth
$N$, the blocks $(J_{m,N})_{jj}=\Omega^{m+j}H$ and
$(J_{m,N})_{j,j+1}=\Omega^{m+j}B$ obey

$$
J_{m+1,N}=\Omega\,U_mJ_{m,N}U_m^\dagger ,
\qquad
\operatorname{spec}\!\left(\Omega^{-m}J_{m,N}\right)=\operatorname{spec}\!\left(J_{0,N}\right).
$$

Every level gains one factor $\Omega$; the proof is algebraic, and unitary
spectral covariance then gives the second statement. Standard ordered Schur
elimination preserves the response of the blocks that were removed. In this
sense *inherit* means: the same dimensionless law, a new absolute register, and
a response that survives elimination of what a given room cannot resolve.

## 3. Why the sphere

If enclosure is the central relation, the sphere is the candidate, and not for
aesthetic reasons: it is the shape that asks the least of the two forces (§2.2).
Three further properties follow from that, and each one has been measured.

**It has an inside without seams.** The boundary of a ball is the minimal closed
surface that separates an interior from an exterior, with no distinguished
points, edges, or corners. Any other closed shape carries structure of its own
— corners, axes, handles — that has to be explained before it can be used. A
sphere needs one number, its areal radius, to say where the inside ends.

**It carries its own clock and its own two ends.** An enclosed region has a
lapse: the rate at which proper time runs relative to the chart. It also has a
bottom end and a top end — the cheapest coherent frequency the room can hold and
the finest structure it can resolve. §6.3 measures both ends on recorded states,
and finds them set by the local space rather than by a universal number.

**It composes by nesting, and nesting has a direction.** A sphere inside a
sphere is again a sphere. The composition is not symmetric: an outer region can
compress an inner one, and a horizon is precisely where the outward path ends
while the inward path remains open. That asymmetry is not decoration; it is a
measurable property of the enclosure, and §6.2 measures it.

Two honest caveats belong here. First, "the sphere" is a statement about the
enclosing surface, not about the contents: a bubble in the Mariana Trench is
still a sphere and the medium changes the balance, not the shape. Second, the
closest proven mathematical sibling of this nesting is *not* spherical: the
cosmohedron theorem (arXiv:2603.03425) proves that the faces of the polytope
behind a cosmological wavefunction are nested **polygons**. §6.4 compares the
two enumerations exactly, and the comparison is instructive precisely because
the two are not the same object. It is also what this nesting looks like after
a full step out of the geometry and into combinatorics (§2.1): the same
wrapping relation, counted instead of measured.

## 4. What inheritance means in finite form

Before the measurements, it is worth fixing what "the same law in another room"
does and does not mean in this programme. The ratio structure of §2.3 is what
travels. What does not travel is the absolute register, the occupations, and
the geometric configuration: a child room can have a different scale and a
different state while obeying the same dimensionless relations.

The programme's finite theorem makes this precise for a declared finite coupled
operator family: regional composition, a normalized inherited law, normalized
spectra, and a local response of unresolved regions all hold at every finite
depth. It is a finite statement — it is not a proof of infinite physical time,
and it derives no cosmological abundance.

The inheritance relation is reused inside its stated domain. It is not expanded
into an infinite nest: an unbounded tower with no stated cutoff is treated by
this programme as a dead end, not as a result.

## 5. What has been measured

The programme's measured chain is finite and reproducible. Read in order, it is
the argument that an enclosure can do real work:

1. **A completed finite parent-child pair.** A child collar lies inside a parent
   annulus; independent parent and child canonical geometry evolve as parts of
   one metric under the same action. Four reciprocal physical effects — parent
   source to child probability, child source to parent-annulus probability, and
   the two mean-radius responses — meet their one-percent numerical movement
   targets through the recorded interval. Record:
   `lab/results/development/nsc-nested-parent-child-confirmation-v2.json`.
2. **A finite spherical realization.** On a periodic radial chart, six Gaussian
   fermion columns and the metric evolve together under one action, in an
   explicit conformal gauge with a finite forcing budget. Four matched runs
   reach coordinate time 0.3 without a state reset. A localized region remains
   the leader while normal energy flows across three regional surfaces, with
   space, time, and quadrature indicators below one percent. Record:
   `lab/results/development/nsc-spherical-conformal-episode-v2.json` with the
   transport, clock and curvature successors alongside it.
3. **A local response on the same trajectory.** Reduction to the original
   two-mode observer reproduces the generated occupation to a streamed/full
   discrepancy of 0.00865 percent of an occupation change of 0.07342952.
   Memory, initial exterior drive, and the actual initial cross correlations
   each move the response by more than that discrepancy. Record:
   `lab/results/development/nsc-conformal-local-response-v2.json`.
4. **Source-imbalance controls.** Reversing the source imbalance reverses the
   signed flow at the measured surface, and five-percent perturbations produce
   measured five-percent responses in the recorded coarse domain.

None of these steps is a cosmological observation. Each is a finite realization
with a declared domain, a numerical comparison, and a stated limit. Together
they establish that the enclosure relation is computable and that its response
is measurable, which is what makes the geometric question worth asking.

## 6. The shape's capabilities, measured

This is the part of the programme that the current papers do not yet tell, and
it is the reason this statement exists. The following results were computed on
6 October 2026 and are recorded in the laboratory. Throughout this section the
two forces are the compressive inner contribution and the resisting outer one,
read from the recorded station payloads; §6.5 measures their balance directly.

### 6.1 The cone map: light rides the space

On the recorded horizon-regular Reissner-Nordstrom family, the coupled lapse
and shift were reconstructed from each station's own state and reproduce the
stored mass to `0.000e+00`. The measured facts about light in that room:

- the local speed is **one** everywhere; the null cone does not change its local
  size;
- the chart value of the cone is carried by the shift, not by a dilation:
  the inward and outward chart speeds differ because the state's stress is
  carried in $\beta$;
- the ray-frequency reading at the inner edge stays within $6\times10^{-4}$ of
  one in this flat-slice gauge — the value of light barely changes across the
  ride;
- the chart's outgoing speed crosses zero at $r_+ + 0.00470\ldots0.07422\,r_m$:
  the one-way interface of the *sourced* state sits outside the source-free
  horizon, the offset growing with the energy fraction and falling with the mass
  ratio.

On the inward-march grid, which stops one cell above the inner horizon, the same
offsets are recovered within a grid cell ($+0.0047$, $+0.0078$ and $+0.0151$ for
$\mu=1.040$, $1.010$ and $1.002$). The interface is therefore a property of the
state's stress, not of where the grid was cut.

Record: `lab/results/development/nsc-rn-cone-map-v1.json`, extended by
`lab/results/development/nsc-rn-cone-map-v2.json`.

This is the precise form of "light rides the space": the local speed is a
constant, and the value the chart assigns to light is a property of the room it
is riding.

### 6.2 The one-way enclosure

The enclosure's directionality was measured by building the instantaneous Dirac
rate operator of the final recorded station of each case and splitting its
eigenmodes at the state's own measured trap radius by the signed probability
current.

- For the deep, thick case ($\mu=1.04$, energy fraction $0.010$) **no**
  interior-supported mode carries outward current in any resolved frequency
  band: strictly one way.
- Three cases carry interior-supported transients with net outward current, and
  those live in the **low band** ($|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|<0.5$): the escape
  capability is confined to slow modes that straddle the trap.
- The recorded state's own signed current is outward for $\mu\ge1.01$ and inward
  for $\mu=1.002$: the lightest shell is already past the point of return at its
  final station, the heavier ones are still on their way in.
- Two spectra of the lightest case did not close under the computation's
  wall-clock guard and are recorded as such, beside an eigen-free witness of the
  same state.
- On the inward-march grid the intermediate case reverses: $\mu=1.010$ carries
  no interior-supported outward current in any resolved band (interior maximum
  $-0.356$, against $+0.823$ on the family sample), while $\mu=1.040$ keeps a
  $+0.283$ low-band transient and $\mu=1.002$ again records a timeout.

Record: `lab/results/development/nsc-rn-oneway-v2.json`, extended by
`lab/results/development/nsc-rn-oneway-v3.json`. Sealed version one,
which used speed magnitudes and thereby mislabelled trapped channel content as
outward, is preserved and named in the successor's supersession block.

This is the measured form of "you can be drawn in, but you cannot easily get
out": the classical one-way reading (Bronnikov-Fabris 2005, gr-qc/0511109) is
recovered as a limit — the deep thick case — while the finite operator shows
where the exceptions live.

### 6.3 The two ends of a room

Both ends of a room were read from the same local space. The bottom end is the
angular ground, $\omega_{\text{zero}}=N/r$; the top end is the resolution the
march itself respects, $\omega_{\text{cutoff}}=(N+|\beta|)/\Delta r$; their
ratio is the dimensionless room quality

$$
Q=\frac{N^2\,\Delta r}{r\,(N+|\beta|)} .
$$

Measured on the recorded states: the inner edge holds a room about **nineteen
times sharper** than the outer boundary ($Q_{\text{inner}}/Q_{\text{outer}}$
between 18.9 and 19.9), the inner value moves from $3.9\times10^{-3}$ to
$1.7\times10^{-2}$ across the mass ratio, and the energy fraction changes it only
in the fourth digit. $Q$ is invariant under a self-similar rescaling of the
room.

Record: `lab/results/development/nsc-rn-two-ends-v1.json`. On the inward-march
grid the ratio rises further, to **21.1–25.2×**, with the inner value at
$\mu=1.04$ reaching $2.24\times10^{-2}$: the sharper end sharpens as the
measurement approaches the horizon
(`lab/results/development/nsc-rn-two-ends-v2.json`).

The reading this supports — that Planck-like and zero-like scales are two ends
of the *same local* space rather than two global constants — is a reading. What
is measured is that the two ends are set by the room.

### 6.4 The nested-polygon sibling

The cosmohedron theorem proves that the faces of the polytope underlying a
$\operatorname{Tr}(\phi^3)$ cosmological wavefunction are Matryoshkas:
polygons wrapped into larger polygons, with the maximal ones counted by
$1,2,10,72,\ldots$ (OEIS A177384, from $M=x^2+MM'$) and the facets by the
Hipparchus-Schroeder numbers.

The programme's own nesting is a chain of concentric spherical shells: region
inside child inside parent inside ambient; wrapping a contiguous group of
neighbours is a bracketing of a path, counted by the small-Schroeder numbers
$1,1,3,11,45,197,\ldots$. Both sequences were reproduced exactly from their
recurrences.

The measured relationship: the spherical chain is the **Schroeder skeleton** of
the same nested-wrapping family, while the polygon count is that skeleton raised
by the triangulation freedom — which is why the polygon count grows factorially
and the chain does not. The recursion skeleton matches; the weight type does
not (an additive perimeter energy per bracket on the polygon side, a
multiplicative $1/\Omega$ scale inheritance per level on the spherical side).
No equivalence between the programme's interaction and the cosmohedron is
claimed.

Record: `lab/results/development/nsc-matryoshka-comparison-v1.json`.

### 6.5 The inward march, and the decision it forced

The two-force balance on the recorded family had shown that the outside always
acts — the integrated outer force is 15.6 to 82 percent of the inner force in
every recorded case — and that the balance wall is real but transient, its
persistence falling from about 65 percent to about 31 percent of stations as the
exterior compression strengthens. What it could not do was decide the 2005
regularity criterion: the grid stopped at the horizon midpoint, outside the
inner horizon, leaving a gap of 0.06 to 0.29 $r_m$ never evaluated. An earlier
version of that check declared the interior non-regular on the strength of a
fitted slope extrapolated across the gap; that over-claim was withdrawn, and the
lesson was recorded.

The inward march extended the areal grid to **one cell above the inner
horizon**, keeping the spacing, the packet, and the two-arm march unchanged.
Just above $r_-$ the outgoing speed is still negative, so the chart remains pure
outflow and the extension is admissible. All three cases reached four stations
with no failure file. The result on the evaluated domain:

| mass ratio $\mu$ | inner-region slope minimum at the last station | meets Hayward $g\ge-1$ |
|---|---:|---|
| 1.002 | $-171.6$ | no |
| 1.010 | $-80.2$ | no |
| 1.040 | $-62.3$ | no |

Record: `lab/results/development/nsc-rn-inward-march-assessment-v1.json`.

The compressive force does not flatten at the inner horizon on this branch. The
criterion that could not be decided by extrapolation is now decided by
measurement, and it points at the next question rather than at another
extrapolation.

## 7. What this is not

The programme's discipline is part of its claim, so the limits are stated in the
same voice as the results.

- **No value of $c$ or $\Lambda$ is derived.** The cone map measures the local
  speed and the room's two ends; it does not produce a fundamental constant.
- **No continuum certificate exists.** The numerical indicators satisfy the
  declared one-percent comparisons for the reported effects; they are not a
  total state-error bound against an unknown geometry.
- **No renewal.** "Regeneration" in this programme means maintained structure
  with regional transport over a measured interval. A periodically renewed
  state has not been demonstrated.
- **No identification of our universe with a child room.** The finite
  parent-child construction is a declared finite realization. Mapping it onto
  the observed cosmos is not claimed.
- **The incoming gate stays paused.** The retained source-fixed local gate is
  OPEN with its error-budget campaign paused; it is not resumed here.
- **The one-way statement is an instantaneous spectral measurement**, not a
  time-resolved response, and it is measured on the sourced chart one trap
  offset outside the source-free horizon.
- **The cosmohedron comparison is enumerative.** It identifies the nesting
  skeleton, not the interaction.

## 8. The next question

The inward march turned the regularity question into a branch question: which
source branch brings the inner slope above $-1$? The candidates are the charged
lowest-Landau-Gauss branch, a torsion-corrected (Einstein-Cartan) completion, or
a state-corrected vacuum with a different stress regime. That is a named,
finite question with a computable answer, and it is the next step this programme
intends to take.

The second named question is the one the hypothesis of §2.2 owes. If shape is
selected by cost, anisotropy should track drivers. The four-metric tidal
consumer already shows strong anisotropy growth in driven episodes: at $x=2$,
$T=3$ the radial and angular components are approximately $-5.24\times10^{5}$
and $5.63\times10^{6}$, with the angular component stable under spatial
refinement. That growth is evidence against a naive attractor reading.

The test that decides it is finite: measure whether the anisotropy decays toward
isotropy in an undriven, late-time sector of the same action. On the recorded
driven window the matter-balance departure already falls monotonically in all
nine recorded cases, by 10 to 18 percent — a trend in the predicted direction,
and not yet the test. The thresholds were registered in
`lab/results/development/nsc-rn-isotropy-v1.json` before the relaxation run they
would be read against; that run
(`nsc-rn-relaxation-v1`, $\mu=1.040$, $\varepsilon=0.001$, to $t=80$) has now
been taken and read, in
`lab/results/development/nsc-rn-isotropy-v2.json`. Its departure falls 22.3
percent from first to last station but **not monotonically** — it rises from
0.3839 at $t=20$ to 0.4191 at $t=40$ before falling to 0.3454 — and the tail
stays above half the driven maximum, so the registered verdict is again
`no_relaxation_observed`. The registered refinement indicator is zero on this
layout, so the verdict rests on the half-clause alone.

Two limits on that instrument are measured rather than assumed. The
Reissner-Nordstrom chart is spherically symmetric by construction, so its metric
carries no shape anisotropy at all: the index measures the matter stress, which
is the only anisotropy these recorded states possess. And the frozen-metric arm
— the same action without geometric feedback — reproduces the trajectory to
within $0.0014$ of the index on the driven window and $0.00127$ on the
relaxation campaign, reproducing the $t=40$ rise as well, so the trajectory is
a property of the evolving field and not of the geometry's response. A test of
shape selection therefore needs a
non-spherical sector these runs do not contain: $l\ge2$ metric perturbations on
the same background, which is the next instrument this question names.
