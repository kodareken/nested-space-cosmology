# Recursive Horizons and Black-Hole Cosmology

## Core judgement

Your central idea — that reality may be a recursive process of compression, heating, expansion, cooling, structure formation, and renewed collapse — is not outside the landscape of serious theoretical physics. It sits near several live or historically important research programmes: loop quantum cosmology, Einstein–Cartan torsion cosmology, regular black-hole models, baby-universe production, cosmological natural selection, and conformal cyclic cosmology. What the literature shows, however, is that these programmes each solve only part of the problem. No accepted framework currently gives all of the pieces at once: a nonsingular black-hole interior, a derived parent-to-child transition law, a consistent information and entropy accounting, and a daughter universe that reproduces the observational successes of modern cosmology. citeturn1search2turn2search2turn3search14turn3search20turn10search2turn11search1

The shortest research-grade answer to your one-line question is this: **the “drain” becomes a “faucet” only if collapse enters a quantum-gravity or otherwise ultraviolet-complete transition phase that replaces the classical singularity with a finite, dynamical state and maps that state into a new expanding spacetime.** Several proposals sketch such a mechanism, but none is established for realistic astrophysical black holes, and none is yet integrated with the modern understanding of black-hole information and the precision successes of \(\Lambda\)CDM. citeturn1search2turn3search14turn17search3turn0search5turn4search0

## What standard relativity already supports

For a non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole, the horizon radius is

\[
r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2}.
\]

That part of your write-up is standard. If one then defines a **bookkeeping average density** by dividing the mass by the Euclidean volume of a sphere of radius \(r_s\), one gets

\[
\rho_{\rm avg}=\frac{3c^6}{32\pi G^3M^2}\propto M^{-2}.
\]

So the statement that larger black holes can have lower average density is mathematically correct. What it **does not** mean is that the true interior geometry of a black hole is just an ordinary low-density region, or that our universe is straightforwardly “inside” a Schwarzschild black hole. That stronger identification has been proposed before, notably by Pathria, but later matching analyses found that the junction is not smooth in the simple version of that idea. citeturn9search13turn3search1turn3search5turn2search2

Your treatment of the speed of light is also close to standard relativity, with one important refinement. In general relativity, **local** observers always measure light to move at \(c\). Near a black hole, what changes is not the local law of light propagation, but the causal structure of spacetime. In the “river model” of a Schwarzschild black hole, space can be pictured as flowing inward at the Newtonian escape speed, reaching \(c\) at the horizon and exceeding \(c\) inside, which gives the useful coordinate expression that outgoing light is overwhelmed by inward geometry-flow at the horizon. That is a good intuition pump, but it is a coordinate-based picture, not a new invariant law of nature. citeturn5search0turn19search5

The cosmological analogy is real but weaker than your strongest phrasing suggests. In a Robertson–Walker spacetime, proper distance is \(D(t)=R(t)\chi\), recession speed is \(v_{\rm rec}=H D\), and for an inward-moving photon the proper-distance speed is \( \dot D = H D - c\). At \(D=c/H\), the photon is momentarily stationary in proper distance, which makes the Hubble scale look structurally analogous to the black-hole horizon in the river model. But Davis and Lineweaver emphasise that the **Hubble sphere is not generally an event horizon**: photons can cross it and eventually reach us. So your slogan “a horizon forms when geometry-flow equals local causal speed” is a useful unifying heuristic, but it does not identify the black-hole event horizon and the Hubble sphere as the same kind of object. citeturn6view2turn5search0

On singularities, classical relativity gets you only part of the way. Penrose’s singularity theorem shows that gravitational collapse under broad conditions leads to geodesic incompleteness, which is why classical black-hole interiors contain singular behaviour. But the theorem does **not** tell you the microscopic endpoint, and it does not prove that “infinite density” is physically realised in a completed quantum theory of gravity. This is exactly why so many modern proposals try to replace the classical singularity with a finite-curvature or finite-density core. citeturn2search0turn2search2

## The best existing candidates for turning compression into expansion

The cleanest mathematically explicit bounce equation in the literature comes from **loop quantum cosmology**. In the homogeneous and isotropic models studied by Ashtekar, Pawlowski, and Singh, the classical Big Bang singularity is replaced by a bounce, and the modified Friedmann equation takes the now-familiar form

\[
H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\rho_c}\right),
\]

so that \(H^2\to 0\) at a critical density \(\rho_c\). This is very close to the “maximum compression then re-expansion” structure in your model. The limitation is that this result is best established in symmetry-reduced cosmological settings, not as a proven description of realistic astrophysical black-hole interiors. citeturn1search2turn1search6

A second serious route is **Einstein–Cartan gravity with torsion**, especially in the form developed by Popławski. In that framework, the spin of fermionic matter sources spacetime torsion, and torsion creates an effective short-distance gravitational repulsion at extremely high densities. Popławski explicitly argues that this can avoid singularities both in cosmology and inside black holes, allowing collapse to bounce and generate a new expanding region of spacetime beyond the horizon. Of the existing proposals, this is one of the closest to your “drain \(\rightarrow\) throat \(\rightarrow\) faucet” picture. Its weakness is not internal coherence so much as status: it is a speculative extension of GR without decisive empirical confirmation. citeturn3search2turn3search14turn3search18

A broader family of models replaces singularities with **regular black holes**, often by introducing de Sitter-like cores, modified matter sectors, or effective quantum-gravity corrections. Recent reviews describe this as an active area, not fringe speculation. Related ideas include Rovelli and Vidotto’s Planck stars and black-to-white-hole transitions, where the black-hole interior eventually tunnels or bounces into a white-hole-like phase. These models strengthen your finite-compression intuition, but they do not yet define a consensus mechanism, and white holes remain dramatically less supported observationally than black holes themselves. citeturn2search2turn17search2turn17search3

There is also a more explicitly topological or inflationary line of thought: **baby universes** formed via vacuum bubbles, wormholes, or tunnelling. Reviews of black holes and baby universes describe causal structures in which a new inflating region can emerge and then pinch off from its parent spacetime. This literature shows that the “parent” need not be spatially outside in an ordinary sense; it can instead be related causally or topologically. But these scenarios typically rely on thin-shell constructions, tunnelling, topology change, or special vacuum physics, not merely on ordinary stellar collapse inside classical GR. citeturn10search2turn10search1turn10search13

## The unsolved gates in your framework

The first major gap is the one you identified yourself: there is no accepted **transition map**
\[
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}=F(\mathcal{P}_n,M,J,Q,S_H).
\]
I did not find any established theory that derives a child universe’s constants, matter content, perturbation spectrum, or vacuum sector from the parent black hole’s mass, spin, charge, and horizon entropy. Smolin’s cosmological natural selection assumes that black holes produce new universes and that constants change only slightly between generations, but this is a population-level hypothesis about inheritance and selection pressure, not a microphysical derivation of the inheritance law. The same is true of baby-universe and bounce papers more broadly: they discuss possible mechanisms, not a worked state-to-state map. citeturn3search20turn11search1turn10search2turn3search14

The second gap is **entropy and information**. Bekenstein’s 1973 result and Hawking’s 1975 calculation imply that black holes have thermodynamic entropy proportional to horizon area and emit thermal radiation. Since then, the information problem has become more rather than less central. The modern “islands” and replica-wormhole programme reproduces a unitary Page curve in tractable setups, which strongly suggests that Hawking radiation can, in principle, encode information rather than destroy it. That makes a simple version of “the information goes inward into a baby universe” harder to maintain, because if information also comes out in Hawking radiation, one must prevent duplication and preserve quantum consistency. No consensus framework yet unifies realistic baby-universe creation with the best current semiclassical lessons about information recovery. citeturn1search0turn1search5turn1search5turn7search0turn7search6

The third gap is the status of the **parent universe** and the meaning of “outside.” In black-hole and baby-universe papers, “outside” is often not a place one could reach by flying through ordinary 3-space. Instead, the child region may be causally disconnected, or the throat may pinch off entirely, leaving a separate branch of spacetime. This supports your instinct that “outside” should be interpreted causally or topologically rather than naively spatially. But it also means the parent cannot simply be treated as a larger ordinary universe obeying the same coordinates and constants. citeturn10search2turn10search13turn10search1

The fourth gap concerns the **constants**. Your instinct to ask whether \(c_n\), \(G_n\), or \(\hbar_n\) could differ between pockets is physically legitimate, but modern foundations work would rephrase the question. Varying **dimensional** constants in isolation is partly a matter of units; the sharper question is whether **dimensionless** combinations, such as the fine-structure constant or mass ratios, differ across domains. Reviews by Uzan emphasise that any spacetime variation of constants would signal new degrees of freedom and violations of local position invariance, and present observations place strong constraints on such variations within our observable universe. So if your model wants inherited or mutated constants across pockets, it should formulate them in terms of dimensionless parameters, not just a raw change in \(c\) or \(G\). citeturn16search0turn16search5

The fifth gap is the dark sector. There is currently no accepted derivation of **dark matter** as a gravitational shadow of a parent layer, nor of **dark energy** as simple parent-pressure leakage through horizons. Current reviews still treat dark matter as an empirical missing-mass phenomenon with multiple candidate microphysics or effective theories, and dark energy as either a cosmological constant, a dynamical field, or a sign of modified gravity. None of those frameworks presently derives the dark sector from black-hole reproduction in the quantitative way your proposal would need. Your “crystallisation fraction” is therefore an interesting organising metaphor, but not yet a law of known physics. citeturn14search0turn14search1

## Where observation is already constraining the idea

Any viable recursive-pocket cosmology has to preserve the massive empirical success of the standard internal cosmology. Planck’s final cosmological-parameter analysis found the six-parameter spatially flat \(\Lambda\)CDM model to fit the CMB extraordinarily well, including the acoustic scale and the relative matter and baryon densities. Big Bang nucleosynthesis remains in strong agreement with the broader cosmological picture, and DESI’s BAO measurements provide powerful late-time geometric constraints. So the strongest way to make your framework scientific is not to overthrow \(\Lambda\)CDM inside a pocket, but to let \(\Lambda\)CDM emerge as the **effective interior theory** while your recursive horizon physics operates only at the origin, boundary, or asymptotic recycling layer. citeturn4search0turn4search5turn4search2turn14search1

Black-hole observations also push in a conservative direction. The Event Horizon Telescope found M87* and Sgr A* images consistent with the expected appearance of Kerr black holes in GR, and the EHT has published dedicated tests constraining deviations from the Kerr metric. Likewise, LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA ringdown tests remain consistent with Kerr remnant black holes, and official summaries emphasise agreement with GR in the strong-field regime. These data do not rule out exotic interiors, because most interior models are designed to leave the exterior nearly unchanged. But they do mean that any drain-to-faucet model must reproduce an ordinary Kerr-like exterior extremely well. citeturn15search0turn15search5turn15search6turn8search3

Claims for direct observational hints of horizon-scale new physics remain weak. Searches for gravitational-wave echoes — one of the cleanest proposed signatures of near-horizon modifications — found no significant evidence in O3 binary black-hole events. Likewise, Penrose-style conformal cyclic cosmology claims about concentric low-variance circles in the CMB were challenged by multiple independent reanalyses, which concluded that the reported circles were not statistically anomalous relative to standard \(\Lambda\)CDM expectations. That does not prove such models false, but it means they currently lack decisive observational support. citeturn8search5turn8search1turn12search0turn13search0turn13search1turn13search2

## What would turn your model from a deep metaphor into a physical theory

A strong version of your framework would need to produce at least one **quantitative discriminator** that standard cosmology and standard black-hole physics do not already provide. The most promising categories are a calculable primordial spectrum or non-Gaussian signature from a bounce, a specific and statistically robust deviation in black-hole ringdown or echo physics, a remnant or evaporation signature tied to a nonsingular interior, or a precise inheritance law for dimensionless constants that makes falsifiable population-level predictions. Smolin’s work is relevant here because he explicitly frames cosmological natural selection as something that should make testable predictions, not just philosophical claims. citeturn11search1turn8search5turn1search2

Just as important is falsification. A recursive black-hole cosmology would take a major hit if it could not be embedded consistently in a model that reproduces the CMB acoustic peaks, BBN abundances, late-time expansion history, and dark-matter-driven structure formation. It would also be weakened if increasingly precise EHT and gravitational-wave observations continued to support Kerr exteriors with no statistically significant echoes or other horizon-scale anomalies. At that point, the burden on the theory would shift almost entirely to the unobservable interior, where explanatory freedom is high but empirical leverage is low. citeturn4search0turn4search5turn4search2turn15search6turn8search5

The most disciplined formulation of your idea, therefore, is not “black holes are definitely drains and Big Bangs are definitely faucets,” but rather this: **there may exist a quantum-gravitational transition regime in which some collapsing regions spawn new expanding domains across causal boundaries, while ordinary observers still see nearly standard black-hole exteriors and ordinary \(\Lambda\)CDM cosmology inside their own pocket.** That is scientifically respectable as a conjectural research direction. What it lacks, today, is the derived map that tells us how the rules of the water survive the plumbing. citeturn10search2turn17search3turn15search6turn4search0

## Bottom line

Your framework is closest to a **synthesis of real frontier ideas** rather than a finished theory. Standard GR supports the existence of causal boundaries, horizon thermodynamics, and collapse-driven geodesic incompleteness. Several speculative extensions of GR support finite-density bounces, regular cores, black-to-white-hole transitions, or baby-universe branches. But no accepted theory yet shows, in one mathematically controlled package, how a realistic black hole becomes a realistic new universe **without** losing consistency with information theory, entropy accounting, dimensionless constants, and precision cosmological data. citeturn2search0turn1search0turn1search5turn1search2turn3search14turn2search2turn10search2turn4search0

So the best current answer to **“How exactly does the drain become the faucet without losing the rules of the water?”** is:

**It does not do so in any established theory yet.**  
The most credible possibilities require a nonsingular quantum-gravity transition region, but the exact state map, the information bookkeeping, and the observational signatures are still open problems. Your model is therefore a promising conceptual scaffold for research, but it has not yet crossed the line from physically suggestive architecture to fully predictive cosmology. citeturn0search5turn1search2turn3search14turn16search0turn4search0
