# Part II: Synthesis — Transitions, Observations, and the Eternal Engine

## 4. Parent–Child Transitions and Cosmological Natural Selection

### 4.1 The Transition Kernel

A rigorous model of black-hole reproduction must define a transition map from a collapsing region to a new expanding universe. Instead of a simple function

$$
\mathcal{P}_{n+1} = F(\mathcal{P}_n, M, J, Q, S_{\rm H}),
$$

one needs a quantum channel

$$
V_X : \mathcal{H}_{\rm collapse}(X, \theta_n) \rightarrow \mathcal{H}_{\rm Hawking}(X, \theta_n) \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\rm child}(X, \theta_{n+1}),
$$

where $X = (M, J, Q, S_{\rm H})$ collects macroscopic charges, $\theta_n$ are dimensionless physical constants in pocket $n$, and $\mathcal{H}$ denotes Hilbert spaces of states. To respect unitarity one demands

$$
V_X^{\dagger} V_X = \mathbb{I}.
$$

The child state is obtained by tracing out Hawking radiation:

$$
\rho_{\rm child} = \operatorname{Tr}_{\rm Hawking}[V_X \rho_{\rm collapse} V_X^{\dagger}].
$$

The finite entropy $S_{\rm H}$ bounds the size of the child's Hilbert space.

### 4.2 Information Conservation and the Page Curve

Black-hole information arguments and replica-wormhole techniques indicate that Hawking radiation can encode information in a unitary way. Therefore a baby universe cannot simply steal information that must also appear in Hawking radiation; there must be correlations between the two outputs. An entropy relation reads

$$
S_{\rm rad} + S_{\rm child} - I(\text{rad} : {\rm child}) \le S_{\rm H},
$$

where $I$ is mutual information. This ensures that no more information emerges than can be stored on the horizon [5].

### 4.3 Mutation of Constants and Cosmological Natural Selection

Dimensionless physical parameters — such as the fine-structure constant $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c)$, mass ratios $m_e/m_p$, or the combination $G m_p^2/(\hbar c)$ — may vary between universes. A plausible inheritance law could be

$$
\theta_{n+1} = \theta_n + \frac{1}{\sqrt{S_{\rm H}/k_B}} \, A(X) \, \xi,
$$

with $\xi$ a random vector and $A(X)$ capturing dependence on horizon properties. Larger horizons produce smaller mutations (because $\sqrt{S_{\rm H}}$ appears in the denominator). This idea resembles Smolin's cosmological natural selection: universes that produce more black holes have more descendants; constants mutate slightly; and an ensemble could be biased towards parameters that maximise black-hole formation.

## 5. Observations and Tests

### 5.1 Age and Parameters of Our Universe

Measurements of the cosmic microwave background by NASA's WMAP and ESA's Planck satellites refine the age of the universe to about 13.8 billion years [8][9]. They deduced this by analysing fluctuations and using Einstein's equations to extrapolate back to the Big Bang.

The observed Hubble constant $H_0 \approx 67$–$70 \; \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ implies a Hubble radius $R_H \approx 14.4 \; \mathrm{Gpc}$ (46 billion light-years). The mass inside this radius at the critical density has a Schwarzschild radius equal to the Hubble radius. Yet the universe is not literally a Schwarzschild black hole because FLRW cosmology has no external asymptotically flat region; every observer sees themselves at the centre of their observable universe.

### 5.2 Parent Horizon and Dark Energy

If a child universe inherits its causal information budget from a parent horizon, one can equate the parent black-hole entropy $S_{\rm BH}$ to the child de Sitter entropy $S_{\rm dS} = 3\pi/(\Lambda\ell_P^2)$. Setting $S_{\rm BH} = S_{\rm dS}$ yields

$$
\Lambda = \frac{3}{r_{\rm s}^2} = \frac{3c^4}{4G^2 M^2},
$$

where $r_{\rm s}$ and $M$ refer to the parent black hole. If the parent has mass $\sim 5 \times 10^{22} M_\odot$, this gives $\Lambda$ of order $10^{-52} \; \mathrm{m^{-2}}$, close to the observed cosmological constant. This relation shows how a huge parent horizon can produce a tiny vacuum energy in the child universe and recasts dark energy as horizon memory rather than a fundamental vacuum energy.

### 5.3 Dark Matter and Baryons

The abundance ratio $\Omega_{\rm DM}/\Omega_b \approx 5.4$ is an empirical outcome of particle physics and cosmology, not obviously tied to horizon thermodynamics. In this framework dark matter arises from physics of reheating or a relic sector; it is not simply leaked parent energy. A plausible explanation is that baryon and dark asymmetries are generated by similar mechanisms, leading to $\eta_X \sim \eta_b$ and $m_X \sim 5 m_p$, so that $\Omega_{\rm DM}/\Omega_b \approx m_X\eta_X/(m_p\eta_b) \approx 5$.

### 5.4 How to Test the Model

A realistic model must reproduce the $\Lambda$CDM observables: cosmic microwave background anisotropies, light element abundances, large-scale structure, etc. Predictions that could differentiate the "eternal engine" include:

- **Dark-energy relation**: $\Lambda \propto M^{-2}$ for the parent horizon. If future cosmological measurements reveal a dynamic vacuum energy inconsistent with $\Lambda$ being constant, the simple saturation rule fails.
- **Spectral index and perturbations**: The child universe's primordial spectrum must match observations ($n_s \approx 0.965$, $A_s \sim 2.1 \times 10^{-9}$). Inflation or another mechanism must generate these numbers.
- **Horizon entropy matching**: If the child de Sitter entropy does not equal or fall below the parent horizon entropy, the model violates the entropy bound.
- **Black-hole fertility**: Simulations could vary constants and evaluate how many black holes form. If our constants do not sit near a local maximum of black-hole production, cosmological natural selection would be disfavoured.

## 6. The Eternal Engine Versus Infinite Space

### 6.1 Process Not Size

The "infinity" relevant to cosmology need not be infinite spatial extent. Instead, infinity can manifest as an endless process of compression and expansion. Each universe pocket is finite and has a finite horizon entropy, but the series of pockets can be infinite. This notion of temporal infinity avoids the philosophical pitfalls of an infinite static space.

### 6.2 Dimensionless Ratios Rather Than Unit Numbers

We should not ask why $c = 299{,}792{,}458 \; \mathrm{m/s}$ or why absolute zero is $-273.15^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. These values depend on human units. The meaningful questions concern dimensionless combinations and causal structures:

- **Why is there a finite invariant causal speed?** Because horizons require a finite causal cone; without finite $c$ there are no causal boundaries.
- **Why is there a lower bound on temperature (absolute zero)?** Similarly, bounce cosmology posits an upper bound on density/curvature ($\rho \le \rho_c$) that plays an analogous role.
- **Why is the cosmological constant so small in Planck units** ($\Lambda\ell_P^2 \sim 10^{-122}$)? Because it may be the inverse of a huge parent horizon entropy.

### 6.3 Pressure-Cooker Analogy

A "pressure cooker inside pressure cookers" is a good metaphor for nested horizons. A local region collapses when internal pressure can no longer support its own weight. It pinches off causally, forming a black hole. The exterior universe remains intact; only the local domain evolves into a new causal region. This is like a sealed inner pot collapsing within an outer pot. The inner pot does not burst the outer one; it creates a sealed partition. After the pinch-off and bounce, the interior can expand into a new universe pocket.

## 7. Conclusion

The ideas explored here replace the picture of an infinite static universe with an eternal dynamical engine. Horizons are not just boundaries that hide singularities; they behave as thermodynamic membranes encoding finite information and determining how matter and energy are recycled. Black holes are local drains — regions where geometry flow overtakes causal speed and compresses matter into a horizon state. In some quantum gravity scenarios, the collapse halts at a finite density and bounces into a new expanding domain. Cosmological horizons act as global versions of these membranes, with their own temperatures and entropies.

A complete theory must supply a transition kernel describing how a parent black-hole interior produces a new universe and how the constants of nature change. It must obey unitarity, respect the entropy budget, and reproduce the successful predictions of $\Lambda$CDM. Conceptually, the model explains dark energy as horizon memory rather than a mysterious vacuum energy, and casts the cosmos as a finite gradient chamber that forms, cools, crystallises, and eventually recycles into new chambers. Infinity thus emerges from an endless process rather than an unbounded spatial extent.
