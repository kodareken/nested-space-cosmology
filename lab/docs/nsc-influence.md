\[

## A normalized closed-time-path functional

Let C_ij=<c_j dagger c_i> be a Gaussian fermionic covariance, 0<=C<=I. For
one-particle unitaries U_plus and U_minus generated by the two histories,

\[
\boxed{Z[+,-;C]=\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm Fock}(U_+\varrho U_-^\dagger)
=\det(I-C+C U_-^\dagger U_+),\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm IF}=-i\log Z.}
\]

The Fock-to-one-particle trace identity is established mathematics; see
[Klich](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0209642). Here it is implemented for the
actual geometry vertex and independently checked against an explicit
eight-dimensional Fock-space calculation with noncommuting mixed-state data,
as well as a pure occupied orbital. No large Fock-space construction is used
for the field calculation.

Unitarity and positivity imply

\[
Z[H,H]=1,\qquad Z[-,+]=Z[+,-]^*,\qquad |Z|\le1.
\]

The logarithm is taken on its local branch near identical histories.
Exactly zero overlap is valid but has no logarithmic branch. LU log-diagonal
evaluation distinguishes a finite logarithm with an underflowed displayed
amplitude from a singular overlap. A global determinant phase or recursive
infinite-dimensional continuation is not inferred from this local branch.

## Force, response and noise are different variations

Use H_int=+J V and J_delta=J_plus-J_minus. On the physical branch,

\[
\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm IF}}{\delta J_\Delta(t)}=-\langle V(t)\rangle.
\]

For Hermitian bilinears A and B, the Gaussian connected correlator is

\[
\langle AB\rangle_c=\operatorname{Tr}[C A(I-C)B].
\]

Consequently,

\[
\chi_{AB}^{\rm ret}(t,s)=-i\theta(t-s)
\operatorname{Tr}[C[A(t),B(s)]],
\]

\[
N_{AB}(t,s)=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}[C A(t)(I-C)B(s)].
\]

The difference/difference Hessian is iN; the difference/common-source Hessian
is minus the retarded susceptibility in these conventions. Additional
explicit second derivatives of H supply contact terms for nonlinear metric
coordinates. The chosen inverse-radius coordinate makes H affine in J.

The calculation differentiates the two ordered branch histories themselves.
A common source after the observation cancels between the branches, giving
zero response. A prior source agrees with the commutator calculation. This
tests temporal causality of the finite state functional; it is not a proof
of continuum spacelike microcausality or a solved detector model.

For real smearing coefficients, the symmetrized noise matrix is positive:
it is the real part of the Gram matrix of (I-C)^(1/2) A_i C^(1/2). Direct
eigenvalue tests confirm this for the geometric force at several times.
At equilibrium, positive-frequency weights satisfy

\[
N(\omega)=-\coth(\beta_T\omega/2)\operatorname{Im}\chi^{\rm ret}(\omega),
\]

using Fourier convention exp(+i omega t). The zero-frequency elastic thermal
noise is recorded separately; an isolated finite system does not acquire a
reservoir linewidth by assumption. These are matter response/noise kernels,
not the constrained gravitational propagator.

## The fluctuation weight and pair production coincide

For the Gaussian time window f(t)=exp[-t²/(2tau²)], define the Hermitian
smeared generator V_f=int dt f(t)V_H(t). In the energy basis,

\[
(V_f)_{ij}=\sqrt{2\pi}\tau\,
e^{-(E_i-E_j)^2\tau^2/2}V_{ij}.
\]

For the ground-state projector C, its variance is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(V_f)=
\sum_{i-,j+}|V_{ij}|^2\,2\pi\tau^2
e^{-(E_j-E_i)^2\tau^2}
=N_{\rm pairs}^{(2)}/\epsilon^2.}
\]

The first energy moment of these same transitions gives the leading work
coefficient. This is checked against the independent previous
`leading_response` calculation. At tau=0.5:

| Spatial points | Smeared variance / pair coefficient | Work coefficient |
|---|---:|---:|
| 12 | 0.03533458697 | 0.08753737904 |
| 16 | 0.03533459210 | 0.08753739030 |
| 24 | 0.03533459211 | 0.08753739032 |
| 32 | 0.03533459211 | 0.08753739032 |

The unsmeared mean and equal-time variance do not exhibit this convergence:
the bare mean changes from about -0.859 to -1.141 and the equal-time variance
from about 1.475 to 4.564 over the same grids. They are finite-regulator
quantities, not renormalized absolute stress. Time smearing produces a
controlled observable while preserving the separate normalization problem.

Exact time-ordered midpoint products provide another independent history

The smeared generator exponential is not substituted for the complete

\[

\longrightarrow\operatorname{Var}(V_f).
\]

## How this enters the common-action loop

variation and its fluctuations. The static covariant source fixes the
determinant contribution under its declared ultraviolet subtraction. A

\[
