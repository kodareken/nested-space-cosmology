# Finite retained-source response of the nonzero history

This calculation extends the saved group14 response to the sixty nonzero
angular families in the authenticated retained source inventory. It uses the
same nonzero radius-history control, the same fixed upstream construction,
and both original energy signs. The three ell=0 responses are analytically
zero and their baseline allocations remain in the complete baseline.

Each positive-energy/angular family supplies its own original source columns,
coherences, weights and ledger multiplicity. A degree-48 operator on its
original finite energy range is applied to those fixed upstream columns.
The negative-energy partner has the opposite angular sign and the owned
negative source covariance. All120 signed families are distinct; none is
replaced by a factor of two. The existing complete group14 pilot is reused.

Per-family records preserve the operator arrays, all upstream columns, signed
matter changes and retarded derivatives. Their digests bind the history,
channel parameters, source inventory, code and predecessor records. The
runner resumes completed families and rejects stale or altered records.
Every new family has a90 CPU-second cap; the outer budget stops dispatching
at completed family boundaries. The final record is emitted only when all60
required families are present.

The final sum adds the complete incoming baseline and the local/reference
geometric change once. Source identity and arithmetic replay are checked
without repeating the field PDE solves. Replay does evaluate the existing
homogeneous reference from the saved upstream columns.

This is a finite retained-quadrature response and its one-amplitude retarded
derivative, not a closed incoming gate. Missing low/subgap source accuracy,
field/node/interpolation error, changed-history infinite tails and residual
control between incoming nodes remain OPEN. The raw N,beta approximants are
reported as such. No parameter refit, empirical stress correction, new
Gamma_rest or metric timestep is performed.
